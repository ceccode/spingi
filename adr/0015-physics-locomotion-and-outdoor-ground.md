# 0015: Physics locomotion and outdoor ground in the simulator

Status: Accepted · Date: 2026-11-01 · Milestone: M4.0 · Amends [0006](0006-kinematic-sim-adapter-with-g1-model.md) · Supersedes the simulator part of [0013](0013-locomotion-vendor-controller.md)

## Context
The kinematic base of ADR-0006 slides a robot with frozen legs along a straight line: it cannot drift, slip, get stuck or fall, and the floor is flat and the same everywhere. That was enough to build the runtime, but it hides what a walking robot does to a plan, and it cannot show the ground that is the reason to pick a quadruped: asphalt, gravel, dirt, grass, kerbs, ramps. ADR-0013 (proposed) kept the kinematic base and admitted a walking policy "only to study terrain". Published walking policies now run in MuJoCo on a laptop CPU: the G1 velocity policy of unitree_rl_mjlab and the Go2 policies of rl_sar (HIMLoco, robot_lab), all Apache-2.0, all small multilayer perceptrons.

## Decision
**A second locomotion mode, `physics`** (`spingi run|bench --physics`, `SessionConfig.locomotion`). Learned policies stand in for the vendor's controller: they read the body (gyroscope, gravity, joints, last action) and a velocity command every 20 ms, and a PD loop tracks their joint targets at 200 Hz. `walk_to` becomes a closed loop on the simulated pose (`follow`): turn towards the waypoint if it is behind, walk while steering, sidestep the last centimetres, turn to the final heading, with ramped commands. That loop is the one the hardware adapter will run above the vendor's controller. The Go2 walks with HIMLoco, which handles rough ground but barely turns on the spot, and manoeuvres (turns, slow moves) with robot_lab (`GaitSwitch`); the G1 has one policy. Weights are vendored under `runtime/sim/policies/` as numpy arrays with their licenses; inference is numpy only.

**What a walking robot reports.** An obstacle pushed with more than 40 N for 0.1 s blocks the walk (`blocked_by`); no progress for 4 s is `stuck`; a base tilted more than 60 degrees or lower than the policy's fall height is a fall: the adapter e-stops itself and raises `RobotFell`, the Executor records `robot.fell` and ends the run as `estop`, and the gate counts it as a fatal run and as a fall. A run's seed moves the start by up to 3 cm and 3 degrees, so repeated physics runs differ as repeated real runs do.

**Ground as data.** A scene's `terrain` section gives surfaces (indoor floor, concrete, asphalt, gravel, dirt, grass) with their grip and roughness, regions where they apply, ramps and raised areas. Roughness lives in MuJoCo height fields at 10 cm cells; kerbs, pavements, docks and ramps are solid boxes. The surface decides the grip and the ground the contact stiffness: the policies' own soft feet sink below a height field and catch between cells. Kinematic mode follows the ground's height. Episodes record joint angles, base height and tilt, and the ground (`terrain.json`); the viewer draws the ground and moves the legs.

**Kinematic stays the default**: exactly repeatable and faster, for golden episodes, contract tests and quick benches. Physics runs about 40 times faster than real time on a laptop CPU; the lab inspection round takes about 2 s instead of 1.2 s.

## Consequences
The runtime is now exercised against a robot that walks: plans meet drift, blocked paths and falls, and the gate measures them. Plans and scenes have to be written for walking robots, and the scenes in the repository were: a kerb is approached square on at 0.6 m/s, routes keep about 0.6 m from obstacles, and an intermediate waypoint faces the next one (`navigate`). The policies are stand-ins, not the vendor's firmware: the G1's was trained on flat ground and falls on any step of 6 cm or more and on rough dirt, which is pessimistic for the real robot; the Go2's were trained in other simulators. Results speak about how the runtime copes, not about how well a Unitree walks; the hardware decision of ADR-0013 is unchanged. Physics differs slightly between machines, so physics tests assert outcomes measured as robust (8 out of 8 trials) and golden episodes stay kinematic. A 30-run physics bench of the yard patrol takes about two minutes.
